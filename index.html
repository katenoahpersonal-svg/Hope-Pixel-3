World 01 · Identity · Studio · 2023 — now

Hope in Print

A studio built on the belief that hope is printable.

Client
Small businesses, ministries & nonprofits
Role
Founder · Creative direction · Everything with a plug
Field
Brand, web, print, film & marketing

Hope in Print began as a question: what does hope look like when you can hold it? The answer became a studio — one that carries small organizations’ stories across every surface they live on, from pressed paper to glowing screen, and makes each one feel lit from inside.

The challenge

Small businesses, ministries and nonprofits rarely get one team that can hold their whole story — the brand, the website, the print piece, the film, the campaign. They get fragments from different vendors, and the story falls apart between them.

Strategy

Be the through-line. One studio, one visual system per client, every deliverable speaking the same language — from business card to storefront to Sunday-morning screen. The studio manages the full client lifecycle: discovery, proposal, scope, timelines, feedback, production, launch and post-launch support.

Process

  1. Discovery — listening before designing; goals, audience, constraints.
  2. Proposal & scope — clear deliverables, honest timelines.
  3. Identity & system design — logo, palette, type, voice.
  4. Production — web builds, print files, film edits, campaign creative.
  5. Launch — deployment, proofing, go-live support.
  6. Post-launch — support, iteration and the next chapter.

Tools & platforms

  • Adobe Photoshop
  • Illustrator
  • InDesign
  • Premiere Pro
  • Figma
  • Canva
  • Shopify
  • BigCommerce
  • Squarespace
  • Wix
  • HTML / CSS / JS

The solution

Cohesive brand identities and responsive websites that balance visual storytelling with usability and real business goals — plus art direction, photography, video editing and post-production, interactive PDFs, digital devotionals and narrative-driven content. Careful, luminous design that carries an idea further than it could travel alone.

Impact

  • A working studio serving multiple clients since January 2023.
  • One consistent system per client across digital and physical touchpoints.
  • A studio identity that reads as calm and premium at any size — on paper or on screen.

World 02 · Ecommerce · Web · 2022 — now

Matuska

A multimillion-dollar storefront, rebuilt for clarity.

Client
Matuska Taxidermy Supply Company
Role
Ecommerce lead · UX & front-end · Merchandising
Field
BigCommerce storefront & marketplace operations

A heritage taxidermy supplier with thousands of reproduction lines needed its catalog untangled — and its custom storefront continuously improved without ever interrupting a working, multimillion-dollar business. Kate leads day-to-day front-end and back-end operations: the site experience, merchandising, content and technical problem-solving.

The challenge

Depth was drowning discovery. High-volume product families — hundreds of near-identical reproduction lines — buried the range they were meant to showcase. Every fix had to ship into a live storefront with real daily revenue, on a platform with real constraints.

Strategy

Doorways instead of dumps. Collapse high-volume families into luminous doorway tiles that open into focused, wadeable collections — lake-country fish first, then the whole range — while steadily tuning navigation, product pages and checkout for conversion.

Process

  1. Catalog audit — mapping thousands of SKUs into natural families.
  2. Architecture — doorway-tile system and collection structure.
  3. Build — HTML, CSS and JavaScript through BigCommerce Script Manager and app integrations.
  4. Refine — responsive UI, navigation, product pages, checkout workflows.
  5. Operate — Amazon Seller Central catalog: bulk uploads, GTIN-exempt listings, parent–child variations, compliance and search-suppression fixes.
  6. Iterate — prioritize with leadership, ship, measure, repeat.

Tools & platforms

  • BigCommerce
  • Script Manager
  • HTML / CSS / JavaScript
  • App integrations
  • Amazon Seller Central
  • SEO & merchandising

The solution

A storefront that reads like a museum wing instead of a warehouse manifest — clear doorways, focused collections, conversion-minded product pages — with marketplace operations running in step so the catalog stays healthy on Amazon too.

Impact

  • End-to-end ownership of digital operations for a multimillion-dollar ecommerce business.
  • High-volume reproduction families collapsed into navigable doorway collections.
  • Complex platform problems — from checkout workflows to listing compliance — solved in production.

World 03 · Marketing · Campaigns · 2022 — now

The Signal Season

Integrated campaigns that travel every channel.

Client
Matuska Taxidermy Supply Company
Role
Campaign strategy · Creative · Deployment & analysis
Field
Paid media, email, social & onsite promotion

A seasonal business runs on seasonal signals. Kate plans and executes integrated marketing across Meta Ads, AdRoll retargeting and display, email, social media and onsite promotions — one story per season, translated faithfully into every channel it travels through.

The challenge

Channels drift. An ad says one thing, the email another, the landing page a third — and the customer feels the seams. The work was to align audiences, creative, offers and landing pages so a campaign feels like one voice wherever it finds you.

Strategy

One seasonal narrative, then channel-native translations. Segmented audiences get creative tuned to where they are in the journey; retargeting closes loops instead of shouting; every campaign ends in a performance review that plans the next one.

Process

  1. Campaign calendar — seasons, offers and priorities mapped with leadership.
  2. Audience segmentation — who hears what, and when.
  3. Creative system — ads, email, social and onsite sharing one visual language.
  4. Build & deploy — landing pages, email design, scheduling and launch.
  5. Retarget — AdRoll display and Meta loops for the almost-customers.
  6. Review — results analysis that feeds the next season’s plan.

Tools & platforms

  • Meta Ads Manager
  • AdRoll
  • Email marketing & segmentation
  • Social media
  • Onsite & seasonal promotions
  • Performance review

The solution

A repeatable campaign engine: audiences, creative, offers and landing pages moving in formation, with email directed end-to-end — from planning and design through segmentation, scheduling, deployment and analysis.

Impact

  • Integrated campaigns shipped across paid, owned and onsite channels every season.
  • Email marketing owned from first sketch to results analysis.
  • A feedback loop where each season’s data shapes the next season’s signal.

World 04 · Print · Editorial · 2022 — now

The Paper Field

Catalogs, magazine ads and print that sells.

Client
Matuska & studio clients
Role
Direction · Layout & design systems · Prepress
Field
Print & digital catalogs, magazine ads, collateral

Print is where design has to keep its promises — no patches after launch. Kate directs the production of large print and digital product catalogs and designs magazine advertisements, brochures, banners, packaging and sales materials that hold one brand system across every channel.

The challenge

A catalog is a thousand small decisions pretending to be one book: page planning across a huge product range, product content that has to be right, proofing rounds against real print deadlines — while staying unmistakably on-brand next to the website and the email that point to it.

Strategy

Systems before spreads. A page-planning framework and a design system per catalog, so hundreds of products flow into layouts without hand-carving every page — and a proofing rhythm that catches errors while they’re still cheap.

Process

  1. Page planning — the whole book mapped before the first spread.
  2. Design system — grids, product blocks, typography, ink discipline.
  3. Product content — data, photography and copy wrangled into place.
  4. Layout — spreads built inside the system, art-directed where it counts.
  5. Proofing — creative reviews and stakeholder sign-off.
  6. Prepress — print-ready files, and digital-catalog twins for screens.

Tools & platforms

  • Adobe InDesign
  • Photoshop
  • Illustrator
  • Acrobat
  • Interactive PDFs
  • Photography

The solution

Large product catalogs — print and digital — delivered on deadline with design systems that make the next edition faster, plus trade-publication advertisements and collateral that keep the brand speaking with one voice on paper.

Impact

  • Large print and digital catalogs directed from page plan to print-ready files.
  • Magazine advertisements and seasonal materials across trade publications.
  • A consistent brand system maintained across print, web, email and social.

World 05 · Interactive · 3D web · 2025

Hope Pixel

A two-room dream gallery in a single file.

Client
Hope in Print — self-initiated
Role
Creative direction · CSS-3D engineering
Field
Immersive web, zero dependencies

Hope Pixel is a creative-studio site built as two connected rooms you walk through — an entire gallery, its lighting and its parallax, inside a single HTML file with no libraries at all.

The challenge

Restraint as spectacle: how much dimension can pure CSS carry before anyone notices there is no engine underneath?

Strategy

Immersion by default — no popups, no mode switches. The whole site is the gallery; every pixel of chrome that could be removed, was.

Process

  1. Room sketches — two spaces, one continuous camera idea.
  2. CSS-3D scene graph — perspective, transforms, layered depth.
  3. Light & parallax — gradients and motion standing in for a renderer.
  4. Performance — one file, no dependencies, nothing to wait for.

Tools & platforms

  • HTML
  • CSS 3D transforms
  • Vanilla JavaScript
  • GitHub Pages

The solution

An immersive two-room gallery that loads instantly, works everywhere CSS does, and proves the studio’s thesis: craft carries further than tooling.

Impact

  • A complete immersive site in one HTML file, zero dependencies.
  • The foundation of the studio’s experimental practice — which grew into this atlas.

World 06 · Product · Motion · 2026

Clover

An organic social garden, designed for humans.

Client
Self-initiated product
Role
Product design · Front-end build
Field
Social product & interaction design

Clover is a social feed that grows like a garden instead of an engine. No engagement bait, no infinite noise — a natural feed with hearts shaped like clover leaves and a signup flow designed so only humans can pass through it.

The challenge

Design a social product without the machinery that makes social products feel bad — and make calm feel alive rather than empty.

Strategy

Borrow rhythm from meadows: content breathes, saves feel like pressing flowers, search wanders rather than ranks. Let the interface teach the pace.

Process

  1. Grove — the first prototype: a feed with no counters.
  2. Meadow — the second: motion language and the clover-heart mark.
  3. Clover — the third: profiles, saving, search and bot-proof onboarding.

Tools & platforms

  • HTML / CSS / JavaScript
  • SVG motion
  • Vite
  • Design system of one

The solution

A working feed with profiles, saves and search that stays gentle under the thumb — three iterations toward calm, each one shippable.

Impact

  • A living thesis in humane product design — Grove became Meadow became Clover.
  • Bot-proof onboarding designed for people, not metrics.

Process

Six phases, whether the deliverable is a storefront, a catalog or a campaign. Wonder is welcome; surprises are not.

  1. Discover

    Listen first. Goals, audience, constraints and the thing you’re actually trying to say — gathered before anything gets designed.

  2. Define

    Scope, timelines and success measures put in writing. The campaign calendar — or the page plan — becomes the contract.

  3. Design

    Strategy translated into visual language: identity, UX, layouts, systems. Reviewed with stakeholders while changes are still cheap.

  4. Build

    Storefronts, catalogs, campaigns, films — produced inside the system, proofed in rhythm, tested where they’ll actually live.

  5. Launch

    Deployment, scheduling, go-live checks. Nothing ships unrehearsed; launch day should be the boring part.

  6. Grow

    Post-launch support and performance review. The results of this season quietly design the next one.
